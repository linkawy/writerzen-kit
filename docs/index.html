WriterZen Guide

How three SEO tools with no public API ended up running from a shell, what you need to run them yourself, and how to get from one seed keyword to a page plan.

Setup takes about fifteen minutes. After that you only come back to the first three sections when a session expires.

The idea

How this works without an API

WriterZen publishes no API. But it uses one itself.

What happens when you open the site
Your browser
→
app.writerzen.net/api/services/…
What we did
wz on a server
→
the very same endpoints

WriterZen's own front end does nothing magical — it sends ordinary JSON requests to their server and renders what comes back. We send those same requests, and identify ourselves the way the browser does: with the session cookie.

The session is the key No API key, no long-lived token. You sign in from your browser and hand the tool the same cookie — it becomes you.
The paths came from their code We downloaded the front end's JavaScript bundle and read every endpoint and field out of it. Nothing was guessed.
Your account, at human pace Your subscription and your data. The tool reads and waits like any other user; it does not hammer the server.

The result: Keyword Explorer, Topic Discovery and Keyword Planner all run from a terminal, and any agent can call them with one command.

Requirements

What you need

Five things. None of them has a workaround.

A WriterZen account whose plan includes the tools There is no trial mode to fall back on. Step 4 shows you how to check your own allowances.
A browser signed in to that account Any browser with DevTools. You will copy two values out of it and nothing else.
A machine with Python 3.7 or newer That can reach app.writerzen.net. No packages to install — wz uses only the standard library.
The writerzen-kit files wz plus the docs. Unpack them anywhere.
Your market: a country name and a language code Such as Germany and de. Set once, never typed again.
A browser extension? Not needed — and it will not work Extensions like Cookie-Editor cannot export the cookie that matters, because it is httpOnly. That was the first thing we tried and it cost us an afternoon. DevTools is the only way.
Setup

Four steps

1

Put the tool on your PATH

cd writerzen-kit
install -m 755 wz /usr/local/bin/wz
wz --help

It will complain that there is no session. That is expected — that is the next step.

2

Set your market once

export WZ_LOC="Germany"
export WZ_LANG=de

Without these the defaults are the United States and English, and you will get numbers about someone else's market without noticing.

3

Copy the cookies out of the browser

  1. Sign in to WriterZen as usual.
  2. F12 → Application → Cookies → https://app.writerzen.net
  3. Copy the values of exactly two cookies: writerzen_session and XSRF-TOKEN
mkdir -p ~/.writerzen && chmod 700 ~/.writerzen
cat > ~/.writerzen/session.txt <<'EOF'
writerzen_session=PASTE_THE_VALUE_HERE
XSRF-TOKEN=PASTE_THE_VALUE_HERE
EOF
chmod 600 ~/.writerzen/session.txt

Both must come from the same browser session. Sign out and they die together.

4

Check that it all works

wz session check
wz limits

session check must print your remaining keyword credits. limits must show a non-zero figure for the tools you intend to use. A zero means that tool is not in your plan, and no amount of retrying will change it.

The session file is a password. Treat it as one.

chmod 600, never commit it, never paste it into a chat or a log. And never share it with another person — each person exports cookies from their own subscription, because sharing a session means sharing the account.

One idea underneath all of it: WriterZen gives you excellent raw material and mediocre conclusions. The volumes, the ranking pages and the SERP-overlap clustering are real. The topic labels, the relevance score and the "golden keyword" columns are not. So let the tool gather, and do the judging yourself.

2

Widen

wz kw "standing desk" --top 40

You get every idea WriterZen has for that seed, with volume and competition. A seed usually returns over a thousand ideas; --top only limits what prints, and the full set is saved as JSON.

Read volume and competition. Ignore cpc (raw) as money — it comes back in a unit roughly 23,000× the dollars the planner reports for the same keyword.

3

Narrow — this step is yours

Out of a thousand ideas, most are irrelevant, off-market, or another company's brand. Cut them down to the twenty or fifty you would genuinely write for. It takes ten minutes, and it is the difference between clustering something meaningful and spending credits on noise.

4

Cluster

wz plan create --name "Desk buying guides" --file keywords.txt

One credit per keyword. Ten keywords finish in well under a minute. What comes back is groups of keywords that share search results — keywords Google treats as the same question. Which means: each group is one page to write, not one keyword.

5

Verify before you trust it

wz plan matrix 179404

This is the step people skip, and it is the most useful one. It prints how many of the top ten results each pair of keywords actually shares:

 123456 
1. ecommerce platform951122A
2. best ecommerce platform581222A
3. shopify pricing118112—
4. online store builder1211011—
5. headless ecommerce221181—
6. shopify vs woocommerce222117—

Read it like this: keywords 1 and 2 share 5 of their ten results, so they are one page. Everything else shares 1–2 with everything else, so those are four separate pages, not a cluster that failed.

The warning sign is a keyword scoring about the same against two different clusters — it sits on a boundary, and that one is yours to decide.

6

Enrich

wz topic "ecommerce platform" --top 30

For each cluster, take its highest-volume keyword and ask what already ranks. You get the real headlines of the pages that rank, their URLs, and the phrases around them. That is your brief: what the existing top results promise, in their own words.

Decision rules

--level is the grouping threshold — how many shared results two keywords need to land together. It defaults to 3. Raise it to 5 for tight, small clusters when you have hundreds of keywords; drop it to 2 when everything comes back unclustered.

Batch size — ten to fifty keywords is comfortable. Beyond a few hundred it gets slow and the credit cost stops being trivial. Split by topic area.

Zero-volume keywords still cluster correctly, because the grouping reads search results rather than numbers. Do not delete them in step 3 just because another tool reported n/a.

Every command saves its raw response in $WZ_OUT. Nothing you paid for is lost to a summary.

Prompts to start with

Copy what you need and change what is in brackets. The order matches the loop above.

Tell me briefly: is the session alive, and are any of the three tools missing from the subscription? If the session is dead, stop and tell me the steps I need to take myself — do not look for another way in, and do not ask me to paste cookies into this chat.

That last line matters: without it an agent may try other routes in, or ask you to paste a cookie into the conversation.

02Keyword researchfree
Run: wz kw "[seed keyword]" Show me the top 40 by volume, with competition. Do not present the CPC column as money, and do not say anything about allintitle or KGR — both are broken in this language. And tell me which market and language these numbers describe.
03Narrowing the listsaves money
From the results above, build a shortlist of the keywords directly relevant to [my business]. Drop other companies' brand names, anything outside [my country], and anything I could not write a page about. Write it one keyword per line to keywords.txt, and before going further tell me how many there are and what it will cost in credits.
04Cluster, then verifythe only command that costs
Run: wz plan create --name "[project name]" --file keywords.txt When it finishes, run: wz plan matrix [project id] Then tell me: - how many clusters came out, and what is in each - which keywords were left unclustered - whether any keyword sits on a boundary — its overlap is close against two clusters

The matrix is the evidence that makes a cluster reviewable instead of something you take on faith.

05A brief per pagefree
For each cluster, take its highest-volume keyword and run: wz topic "[keyword]" Give me, per cluster: - a proposed page title - the 10 most relevant headlines ranking today, with their URLs - the related phrases worth covering Ignore the topic labels and the relevance score the tool prints — they do not hold up in this language.
06Quick checkbefore any session
Run wz session check and wz limits, and tell me in two lines: is the session alive, and how many keyword credits are left?
Warnings

What never to report as a finding

Four things WriterZen prints that look like insight and are not:

  • allintitle and KGR — 0 and ~1.0 for every non-English keyword tested. It reads as "every keyword is a golden opportunity". It is a broken field, not an opportunity.
  • Topic Discovery's topic ranking — it splits a phrase into fragments and reports each fragment's own volume as a topic's. In one run the "top topics" included a bare preposition and a business from an entirely unrelated industry.
  • Its relevance score — 40 of 100 topics in that run scored an identical 87.
  • The AI name a cluster is given — two clearly different clusters were handed the same label. Use the cluster's highest-volume keyword instead.

And one that is a trap rather than a bug: CPC is not the same unit in both tools. Never put Explorer's cpc (raw) and the Planner's dollars in one table.

When it stops working

  • "session has expired" — the one condition that will recur. Redo step 3. Two minutes, and an agent cannot do it for you.
  • Numbers about the wrong country — WZ_LOC and WZ_LANG are unset. Step 2.
  • A zero in wz limits — that tool is not in your plan.
  • An agent reporting results without running anything — ask it to run wz limits and read you the numbers. Real numbers mean it actually reached the tool.

Every number on this page came from live runs, not invented examples. Maintenance details are in SOP.md, and the per-tool reference is in SKILL.md — both in the repository.
